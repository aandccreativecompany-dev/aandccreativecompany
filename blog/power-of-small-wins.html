The Quiet Power of a Small Win

Why the tiny task you keep putting off matters more than the big goal on the wall.

A&C Creative Ventures · Motivation

We tend to save our excitement for the big milestones — the launch, the promotion, the finished project. Research on everyday motivation at work says we've got the scale backwards: it's the small, unglamorous wins that do most of the heavy lifting for how motivated and engaged we actually feel, day to day.

The study that tracked nearly 12,000 daily diary entries

Harvard Business School researchers Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer collected daily diary entries from knowledge workers across multiple companies — nearly 12,000 entries in total — and coded each day against the person's reported mood, motivation, and perceptions. Their finding, published in Harvard Business Review as "The Power of Small Wins," was that progress on meaningful work — even small, incremental progress — was the single strongest predictor of a good day, ahead of recognition, rewards, or clear goals.

#1

Of all the events that could make a workday feel good, "making progress" — even in small, ordinary steps — was the single most common trigger for a positive inner work life, according to Amabile & Kramer's diary study.

They called this the progress principle: people don't need a breakthrough to feel motivated — they need evidence, however small, that they're moving forward. Conversely, the fastest way to kill motivation wasn't a major setback — it was a day with no visible progress at all, even a perfectly fine day otherwise.

Why the small task on your list matters more than it looks like

This reframes the tiny task you keep pushing to tomorrow. It's not "too small to matter" — closing it out is one of the more reliable ways to generate the sense of momentum that makes the next task easier to start. Big goals are motivating in theory; small, visible progress is what actually keeps you moving in practice.

  1. Make progress visible. A checklist, a crossed-off line, a tracker you can see — visible progress registers even when the step itself was small.
  2. Close small loops on purpose. Finishing the two-minute task you've been avoiding often does more for your motivation than starting the big one.
  3. Protect against "no-progress days." On a day everything feels stalled, doing one small, finishable thing resets the pattern.
Do This Today

Find the smallest unfinished task on your list — the one that feels too minor to count — and finish it before you do anything else. Notice what it does to how the rest of the day feels.

Sources

  1. Teresa Amabile & Steven J. Kramer, "The Power of Small Wins," Harvard Business Review (May 2011)
