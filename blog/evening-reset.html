The 10-Minute Evening Reset

A short wind-down routine that makes tomorrow morning dramatically easier.

A&C Creative Ventures · Productivity

Most people's evenings end the same way: scrolling in bed until the eyes physically won't stay open, then waking up already behind. The morning doesn't feel hard because you're lazy — it feels hard because last night handed you a worse starting position than it needed to.

Screens are eating the exact window that should be winding you down

The National Sleep Foundation's 2022 Sleep in America Poll asked Americans directly about their screen habits around bedtime, and the pattern is stark: the closer to bedtime, the more people are still looking at a screen.

Screen Use Approaching Bedtime

National Sleep Foundation, 2022 Sleep in America® Poll. The Foundation flags the "within 1 hour of bedtime" figure as the most concerning for sleep health.

58%

Share of Americans polled who look at a screen within an hour of bedtime — the window the National Sleep Foundation calls most disruptive to falling asleep. Light exposure in this window can delay melatonin release and push your body's clock later.

There's a second piece to this beyond light exposure: consistency itself. A growing body of sleep research — summarized in a National Sleep Foundation consensus statement on sleep regularity — treats a consistent bed and wake time as its own independent factor in sleep quality, separate from total hours slept. An irregular, scroll-until-you-crash bedtime doesn't just cost you light-exposure minutes; it removes the predictable cue that helps your body wind down on schedule at all.

The reset isn't about willpower at 11 p.m. — it's a fixed routine at a fixed time

Trying to use willpower to put the phone down when you're already tired is close to the worst possible moment to rely on willpower — it's precisely when your self-regulation is weakest. The fix is a short, repeatable sequence that runs on autopilot once it's a habit, so there's nothing left to decide at 11 p.m.:

  1. Minute 1–2: Set tomorrow's 3 tasks. (See our 3-Task Rule piece — do it the night before, and morning-you starts already decided.)
  2. Minute 3–5: Physical reset. Lay out anything for tomorrow, tidy one visible surface, put the phone charger somewhere outside arm's reach of the bed.
  3. Minute 6–8: Screens off, lights down. Even a few minutes without a bright screen before lying down helps — this is the minute that matters most per the research above.
  4. Minute 9–10: One calming, non-screen close. A page of a physical book, a few minutes of stretching, or simply lying down and breathing slowly.
Do This Tonight

Set a recurring alarm 10 minutes before your actual bedtime, labeled "reset." When it goes off, phone goes across the room and you run the 4-step sequence above. Same time, same steps, every night — the routine does the willpower work for you.
