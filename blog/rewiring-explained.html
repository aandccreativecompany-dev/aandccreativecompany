Re-wiring, Explained: Can Your Habits and Thoughts Really Change Your Biology?

By A&C Creative Ventures · 4 November 2026 · 3 min read

I find this topic fascinating, and I think it is one of the most freeing ideas around. So many of us walk around with a quiet list of limits. I am like this. I cannot do that. This is my ceiling, and anything beyond it is beyond me.

We put ourselves in a box and then act as if somebody else built it. But what if the walls are softer than we think?

Your brain is not fixed

For a long time the common belief was that the adult brain was essentially finished. We now know that is not the case. The brain can change how it works based on what you repeatedly think, learn and experience. Learning an instrument, picking up a language, or practising a new skill physically alters connections between brain cells. This ability to change is called neuroplasticity.

It continues throughout life. It is stronger in some conditions than others, and it takes time and repetition, but it does not switch off.

What about genes?

Here the story gets more interesting, and it is worth being careful, because this is a subject where it is easy to exaggerate.

Your DNA sequence, the actual letters of your genetic code, does not change because of your thoughts, habits or experiences. What can change is how active certain genes are. The field that studies this is called epigenetics. Chemical markers attached to DNA, such as methylation, can influence whether a gene is switched up or down, and research shows that factors like chronic stress, sleep, diet, physical activity and early-life environment are associated with such changes.

In plain terms: our biology is not separate from our experience. The two are in constant conversation.

What this does and does not mean

Because this area attracts a lot of big claims, here is a sober version.

  • It does mean that habits, stress levels, sleep, movement and relationships are not just "lifestyle" extras. They can influence how your body and brain function.
  • It does mean that you have more room to move than the phrase "that's just how I'm wired" suggests.
  • It does not mean you can think your way out of illness, or that someone who is unwell caused it by having the wrong mindset. Genetics, environment and chance all play large roles.
  • It does not mean change is instant. Much of the human evidence is still developing, and effects are usually gradual and modest rather than dramatic.

Where your own influence lies

Even with those caveats, there is a lot that is within your reach. By changing our habits, by changing the way we talk to ourselves, by managing our stress patterns and by exposing ourselves to new experiences, we are doing things that research links to healthier brain function and healthier gene regulation. We are also, repeatedly, building new neural connections.

That puts a quiet responsibility on you, and it is a hopeful one. How you feel, how far you can push yourself, and how much of your potential you realise are not entirely handed down. They are partly shaped by daily choices.

Practical ways to start

  • Protect your sleep. It is one of the simplest ways to support both mood and learning.
  • Move your body regularly. Even a short daily walk is associated with benefits for brain health and stress.
  • Learn something that feels slightly difficult. Novelty and challenge are food for neuroplasticity.
  • Notice your self-talk. The sentences you repeat about yourself become the paths you walk.
  • Build calming practices into your day: breathing, journaling, time outdoors, conversations with people who make you feel safe.

That is re-wiring, explained simply

You are more adaptable than the box you have been living in. Not infinitely, and not overnight, but really. The question worth asking is not "Am I wired this way?" It is "What do I want to practise?"

Try this today

Choose one tiny daily practice that supports your brain and mood, such as a ten-minute walk, five minutes of journaling, or phone-free time before bed. Do it for fourteen days and note how you feel on day one and on day fourteen.
