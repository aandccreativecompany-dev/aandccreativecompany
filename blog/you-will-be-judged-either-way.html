You Will Be Judged Either Way, So Decide What You Want to Say About Your Own Life

By A&C Creative Ventures · 4 November 2026 · 3 min read

How often have you stopped yourself from doing something you actually wanted to do, because of what people might say? Not once or twice. Probably many times, across many years, in small ways and large ones.

It is worth looking at that habit properly, because once you see how it works, it loses some of its power.

The conditioning started early

Most of us were taught, gently and constantly, to look for approval. We learned to check the room before we spoke. We learned that "good" meant doing what the adults, the teachers, the relatives, and later the colleagues considered right. That is not a criticism of anyone. Belonging matters, and caring what others think is part of how humans live together.

The problem arrives when that instinct stays in charge long after it has stopped being useful, when a thirty- or forty-year-old is still running big decisions past an imaginary panel of critics.

The irony of approval

Here is the uncomfortable truth about trying to please everyone: it does not work. People will have an opinion whether you do what they expect or not.

  • If you stay, someone will have an opinion about why you stayed.
  • If you leave, someone will have an opinion about why you left.
  • If you change, someone will say you have changed.
  • If you do not change, someone will say you never grew.

So the question is not "What will they say?" They will say something. The more useful question is: "What do I want to say about my own life?"

Psychologists also point to something called the spotlight effect: we tend to overestimate how closely other people are watching and judging us. Most people are far too busy thinking about themselves. That does not make criticism disappear, but it does mean the audience in your head is usually bigger and harsher than the real one.

What I do about it

I will share what works for me, and you can take whatever is useful.

First, I try not to make dramatic changes overnight. I understand the pull. Sometimes the motivation is so strong that it feels like the right moment to rebuild everything at once. But big, sudden overhauls tend to burn out fast and lead nowhere. They also tend to attract the most commentary.

Instead, I start small. One step towards what I want, put into my daily to-do list like any other task. It is almost embarrassingly modest, and it is far harder to argue with a small step than a grand announcement.

Second, I stopped giving every opinion equal weight. That was a real shift. Asking for advice is wise. But advice from someone who has done what you want to do is not the same as a remark from someone who has never tried. A friend who knows you well is not the same as an acquaintance with a view. Think about who is actually in a position to know before you let a comment move you.

A simple filter for opinions

When someone offers an opinion about your choices, run it through three quick questions:

  1. Do they know my situation, or only their own?
  2. Do they have experience with what I am trying to do?
  3. Is this about my life, or about their comfort?

If an opinion passes, and it also lines up with what you already sense, take it. If it does not, let it go with thanks. It is your life, after all.

You do not have to prove anything

You do not need to make one giant, dramatic decision tomorrow. You do not need to announce it. You do not owe anyone an argument. What you can do is take one step towards yourself, and then another. That is all.

Try this today

Choose one thing you have been holding back on because of what people might think. Write down the actual step that would move it forward, and add it to today's to-do list. Do not tell anyone until it is done.
